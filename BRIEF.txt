  *Trade practice —* or *Inference —*, or it stays out.

PROVENANCE, WHICH IS THE POINT OF THE PROJECT
  provenance.default.tier plus per-field overrides. Tiers:
    cited      a named source in sources[] backs this
    harvested  a tool fetched it from an open dataset (World Bank, OpenStreetMap)
    tradition  general knowledge of the trade; hedge it in the prose
    inference  this project reasoning from the above; say so in the prose
    field      somebody stood there (nothing carries this yet)
  In text.story mark each stretch inline: plain prose = cited; *Trade practice —* ;
  *Tradition holds —* ; *Inference —*.
  Set needs_verification: true and confidence: "medium"/"low" where you are unsure.

WHAT THIS SUBJECT PUNISHES
  Numbers. The headline figures in this field — "X million medical travellers a year",
  "up to 80% cheaper" — come from market-research summaries that resell each other and
  count hospital visits by foreigners, which includes every expatriate with a chest
  infection. Do not repeat them as facts. Where a number is worth carrying, carry the
  narrow one: a named hospital's count of its own patients, a regulator's register, a
  national statistics office, a peer-reviewed study. Say what it counts.

  Prices. A price is a number somebody PUBLISHED, with a date and an inclusion list.
  Read it off the page yourself. The exclusion list is usually the story.

  Law. One named instrument, one date, one jurisdiction per legality row. Nothing here
  is legal advice and nothing here is medical advice; the pages describe, and never tell
  a reader what to do about their own body.

THE ARGUMENT IS REPORTED, NEVER JOINED
  Cheaper against riskier. The queue at home against the fee abroad. The state that will
  not pay against the state that will not allow. A university hospital and a back-street
  hair clinic get described the same way, in the same voice, with the same evidence rules.
